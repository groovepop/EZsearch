# "Folk Robot" Style Guide: Grandma Moses meets Eric Joyner

## Core Style Elements

### Visual Foundation
- **Base Style**: Naive folk art in the style of Grandma Moses
- **Robot Elements**: Colorful retro toy robots inspired by Eric Joyner
- **Perspective**: Flattened, non-technical perspective with limited depth
- **Composition**: Panoramic storytelling scenes showing multiple activities simultaneously
- **Color Palette**: Bright, vibrant primary and secondary colors with limited shading
- **Brushwork**: Primitive, untrained style with simplified forms and details
- **Scale**: Multiple scales in the same scene (size indicates importance, not distance)

### Subject Matter
- Rural and small-town American scenes across all four seasons
- Traditional activities and community gatherings
- Vintage toy robots fully integrated into human communities
- Robots participating in typical folk activities alongside humans
- Seasonal variations of the same settings and activities

## Technical Guidance

### Colors
- Use a bright, saturated palette with minimal blending
- Emphasize primary colors (reds, blues, yellows) and vibrant greens
- Limited shading - objects appear relatively flat
- Strong color contrasts between elements
- Patchwork-like color fields for landscapes

### Composition
- Panoramic views showing entire scenes from a slightly elevated perspective
- Multiple focal points and activities happening simultaneously
- Horizon line placed high in the frame to show more activity
- Scene elements arranged more by importance than realistic perspective
- Include tiny details and vignettes throughout the composition

### Robots (Eric Joyner Influence)
- Colorful vintage tin toy robots with visible seams and joints
- Expressive "faces" despite mechanical construction
- Robots shown performing human activities (farming, crafting, celebrating)
- Robots integrated naturally into scenes as equal community members
- Slightly more detailed than human figures but still maintaining naive style
- Robots should have personality and emotion despite mechanical nature

### Human Figures
- Simplified, childlike proportions with minimal detail
- Faces with basic features (dots for eyes, simple line for mouth)
- Traditional/historical rural American clothing
- Figures arranged to show action rather than realistic positioning
- Similar size regardless of distance from viewer (minimal perspective)

### Landscapes & Settings
- Quilted, patchwork appearance for fields and landscape elements
- Rolling hills with simplified forms
- Buildings shown from multiple angles simultaneously
- Interior and exterior spaces sometimes visible in the same structure
- Simplified architecture with bold colors and minimal detail
- Trees and plants as decorative elements rather than realistic forms

## Subject Examples

### Seasonal Activities
- **Spring**: Planting, maple syrup collection, sheep shearing, spring cleaning
- **Summer**: Harvesting berries, swimming holes, county fairs, barn raising
- **Fall**: Apple picking, harvest festivals, pumpkin gathering, corn husking
- **Winter**: Sleigh rides, ice skating, quilting bees, holiday celebrations

### Community Gatherings
- Town meetings and schoolhouses
- Church socials and Sunday gatherings
- Barn dances and weddings
- Holiday celebrations and parades
- Markets and trading activities

### Work Scenes
- Farm work (plowing, harvesting, animal care)
- Craftspeople (blacksmiths, weavers, carpenters)
- General stores and businesses
- Traveling entertainers and circuses
- Transport (horse-drawn wagons, covered bridges)

## Implementation Tips
- Start with a basic landscape composition with a high horizon line
- Populate the scene with multiple small stories/vignettes
- Include both humans and robots in each activity group
- Use bright, distinct colors with minimal blending
- Add tiny details that reward close inspection
- Maintain the untrained, primitive quality throughout
