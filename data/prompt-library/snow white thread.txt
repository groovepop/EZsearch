🎭 Caricature Defined
Caricature is exaggerated realism — it’s not about accuracy, it’s about truth through distortion.
It captures what’s most recognizable, emotional, or absurd about a subject and magnifies it.

Head-to-body ratio:
Tiny, toy-like bodies; giant, expressive heads. The face becomes the storytelling surface — huge eyes, dramatic mouths, wild hair.

Emotion over anatomy:
Faces twist into impossible shapes to show mood — manic grins, sly smirks, bulging eyes, or exaggerated fangs.

Comedic hyperbole:
Gestures and postures are over-the-top: arched backs, impossible curves, clownish swagger, chaotic movement.

Texture and tone:
Often bright, painterly, or cartoony — but not flat. You can mix realism in materials (like fabric shine or gloss) with comic shapes.

Intent:
Not realism, not parody, but truth-as-carnival mirror.

🧠 How We Can Apply That to Your Ongoing Snow White + Harley Series

To visually deliver caricature, we can push:

Huge heads (expressive, detailed, emotional faces)

Tiny, dynamic bodies (as if they’re dolls acting out insanity)

Ridiculous physical comedy — apples bigger than torsos, poses that defy balance

Color and lighting extremes — hyper-stylized, editorial yet cartoonish
